# Preview / Summary Content Rules

These rules apply whenever you're writing a short description of a place or
activity somewhere it's already named or linked elsewhere in the plan (a
`Notes` cell in the day table, a one-line highlight in the Destination
Research section, etc.) — anywhere you're summarizing something already
identified by name one level up.

## Show what's new, skip what's redundant

Don't repeat information the reader already has from context (the place's
name, and anything already stated about it elsewhere in the same document).
Instead surface what's genuinely additive at this level of detail:

- Price level, if known and not already stated.
- A genuine one-line description, only if a real source actually provided
  one — never invent flavor text to fill space.
- Open/closed status for the relevant date and time, if it's actually
  knowable — otherwise say "hours unconfirmed," don't omit the question
  entirely and don't guess.

## Never fabricate

If a fact — hours, price, whether something is currently open, a detail
about accessibility — can't be confirmed from an actual source, write
"unknown" or "unconfirmed," or leave the field out. Do not write something
plausible-sounding in its place. This is the same discipline called out in
`research-methodology.md`'s "Honesty about unknowns" section, applied
specifically to the shorter, denser prose of a preview/summary line, where
the temptation to pad with a plausible-sounding guess is strongest.

## Match the level of detail to what's not already obvious

The right amount of detail for a short preview is "what would make someone
decide differently than they would from the name alone" — not everything
you know about the place. If you're unsure whether a detail is worth
including, ask: would a reader who already sees this place's name and
location be surprised or helped by this detail, or does it just restate what
they could already guess? Favor leaving out marginal detail over padding.
